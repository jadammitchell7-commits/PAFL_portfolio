    Physical literacy

    "Individuals who are physically literate move with competence and confidence in a wide variety of physical activities in multiple environments that benefit the development of the whole person."

    Physical and Health Education Canada, What is Physical Literacy (2017)

    Components of fitness

    Health-related
    Cardiorespiratory endurance
    Your heart and lungs' ability to fuel activity over time.
    Muscular strength
    The most force a muscle can produce in one effort.
    Muscular endurance
    How long a muscle can keep working without tiring.
    Flexibility
    The range of motion available at a joint.
    Body composition
    The relative makeup of the body — not a target number, a training variable.
    Skill-related
    Agility
    Changing direction quickly while staying in control.
    Balance
    Keeping your centre of gravity over your base of support.
    Coordination
    Using senses and body parts together smoothly.
    Power
    Strength applied quickly — force over time.
    Reaction time
    How fast you respond once a signal appears.
    Speed
    How fast you can move from one point to another.
